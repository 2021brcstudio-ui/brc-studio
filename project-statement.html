Project Statement

Honing the Edge / The Surrogate and the Exception

*Honing the Edge* and *The Surrogate and the Exception* are two unfoldings of a single project under different spatial conditions.

The project examines how the same group of objects acquire new identities within different systems, and what remains untranslatable throughout processes of transformation.

The project is not completed in advance and subsequently placed into space. Rather, it gradually takes shape within the existing orders, languages, and material relations of each site.

At DNArt, *Honing the Edge* confronts the way the exhibition system reorganizes objects drawn from everyday reality. Once entering the white cube, objects temporarily detach from their original functional relations and acquire new modes of viewing through the mechanisms of display. Materials, structures, and forms become the primary grounds of perception.

Yet this transformation is never complete. The traces of production, histories of use, and material limitations carried by the objects continue to exist, producing an ongoing tension with the exhibition system. *Honing the Edge* does not refer to a finished condition of sharpness, but rather to a continuous process of cutting and wearing away: objects are repeatedly named, classified, and viewed, while simultaneously revealing the excess that the exhibition system cannot fully absorb.

At Moshikou, *The Surrogate and the Exception* enters an already operating structure of reality. Local experience, commercial relations, historical architecture, and everyday language form their own order, within which the project seeks its position.

If *Honing the Edge* examines how objects enter the art system, then *The Surrogate and the Exception* examines how art enters an existing system of reality.

Originally scheduled to open on July 10, 2026, *The Surrogate and the Exception* did not take place as planned.

This temporal displacement became part of the relationship between the project and its site. An exhibition does not always unfold according to the timeline of an artistic event; other relations within a space continue to operate according to their own rhythms.

In contrast, the exhibition mechanism at DNArt provides a different condition of absorption: objects, languages, and orders from everyday reality can be reorganized and acquire new relations of viewing within an artistic context. The exhibition will be presented as originally scheduled on July 18, 2026.

The two exhibitions do not attempt to determine which system objects should belong to. Instead, they observe how identities are produced, transformed, and adjusted across different spatial conditions.

The same group of objects occupies different positions in two spaces, generating elements that can never fully coincide.

These differences connect the two exhibitions, keeping the relationship between art, reality, and institutions in a continual state of adjustment.
